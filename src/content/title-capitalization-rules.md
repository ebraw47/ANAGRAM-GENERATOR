# Title Capitalization Rules: APA, Chicago, AP & MLA Compared

Title case is not one universal rulebook. The same headline can be correct in APA and “wrong” in Chicago — and AMA, Bluebook, MLA, the New York Times, and Wikipedia each add their own twists. That is why ranking title-case tools expose multiple style guides, and why this page exists.

## Rules every guide agrees on

1. Capitalize the **first word** of a title or subtitle.  
2. Capitalize **nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs**.  
3. Capitalize short verbs people forget (*is*, *am*, *be*, *do*, *go*).  
4. Lowercase articles (*a*, *an*, *the*) unless they are first or last (last-word rules vary).  
5. Capitalize the first word after a **colon**.

Parts of speech still matter. A short word that looks “minor” may be an adverb or adjective and must be capitalized (*Picking Up the Pieces*, *School’s Out Forever*). Context-aware conversion beats blindly lowercasing every preposition-looking token.

## Where the guides split: prepositions

| Style | Prepositions typically lowercased mid-title | Example feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AMA / APA / AP | Up to **3 letters** (*in*, *on*, *off*, *out*) | Longer ones like *About*, *With*, *From* rise |
| Bluebook / Chicago / Wikipedia | Many up to **4 letters** (*from*, *with*, *over*, *like*) | Softer mid-title look |
| MLA | **All** prepositions | Most lowercase function words |
| NY Times | Selected 2–3 letter list (*at*, *by*, *in*, *for*…); capitalizes *up*, *off*, *out* and longer preps | House-style exceptions |

Short prepositions like *of* and *to* stay lowercase mid-title in nearly every common academic or news style.

## Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS)

| Style | Lowercase | Capitalize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AMA, AP, APA, MLA, Bluebook, Wikipedia | *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so* | — |
| Chicago | *for, and, nor, but, or* | *Yet*, *So* |
| NY Times | *for, and, but, or* | *Nor*, *Yet*, *So* |

## Subordinating conjunctions *as* and *if*

| Style | *as* | *if* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AP, APA, NY Times | lowercase | lowercase |
| Chicago | lowercase | Capitalize |
| AMA, Bluebook, MLA, Wikipedia | Capitalize | Capitalize |

Examples: *Do as I Do* vs *Do As I Do*; *What if We’re Wrong?* vs *What If We’re Wrong?*

## Last word rules

- **AP, Chicago, MLA, NY Times, Wikipedia:** capitalize the last word even if it is minor.  
- **AMA, APA, Bluebook:** no forced last-word rule — a trailing preposition can stay lowercase (*Wish for*).

## Hyphenated compounds

APA often capitalizes both parts of hyphenated major words (*Self-Report*). Chicago and MLA capitalize the first element and subsequent major words, with exceptions for articles and short prepositions after the hyphen. When hyphenation matters for your publisher, convert in that exact style and skim the compound by hand.

## Quick comparison table

| Rule | AMA | AP | APA | Bluebook | Chicago | MLA | NYT | Wikipedia |

## Practical workflow

1. Ask which style your school, publisher, newsroom, or client requires.  
2. Paste the title into our [title case converter](/).  
3. Select **AMA**, **AP**, **APA**, **Bluebook**, **Chicago**, **MLA**, **NY Times**, or **Wikipedia**.  
4. Turn on **Show Explanations** or **Highlight Changes** when you want to learn why a word flipped.  
5. If you are free to choose, convert twice and pick the version that matches your house voice.
