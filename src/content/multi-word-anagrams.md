# Multi-Word Anagrams: How Phrase Anagrams Work

![Multi-word anagrams](/blog/multi-word-anagrams.webp)

A **multi-word anagram** (phrase anagram) rearranges every letter of a word or phrase into **two or more** new words — still using each letter exactly once overall.

Single-word solvers stop at one dictionary entry. Phrase search is what unlocks name play and the classics people share.

## Classic phrase examples

| Subject | Multi-word anagram |
| --- | --- |
| dormitory | dirty room |
| the eyes | they see |
| astronomer | moon starer |
| schoolmaster | the classroom |
| slot machines | cash lost in me* |
| a gentleman | elegant man |

\*Some extended phrases need larger dictionaries or creative spacing — always verify letter counts.

## How phrase search works

1. Find a dictionary word that fits inside your letter pool  
2. Subtract those letters  
3. Repeat until nothing remains  
4. Keep only combinations within your max word count  

That recursive split is why **dormitory** can become **dirty + room** even when no single alternate word uses all nine letters in a satisfying way.

## How to use multi-word mode here

On the free [anagram generator](/#generator):

1. Paste your letters or phrase  
2. Select **Multi-word**  
3. Choose **up to 2** or **up to 3** words  
4. Set **minimum length** to 3 or 4 to skip tiny glue words  
5. Generate and copy phrases you like  

## Tips for cleaner phrases

- Inputs of **8–16 letters** balance creativity and speed  
- Longer names: try 3 words + min length 3  
- If you already know one word in the answer, solve the remainder as a second Exact search  
- Raise min length when results fill with *a*, *to*, *of* noise  

## Multi-word vs subset lists

Subset (“all words”) mode returns individual words of any length. Multi-word mode insists the **whole pool** is consumed by the phrase. Use both: find a strong long word in subset mode, then anagram the leftovers for a handmade phrase.
