# Irregular Periods and Ovulation: What Calculators Miss

Calendar-based tools work best with fairly regular cycles. Tommy’s defines irregular periods as gaps often under 21 or over 35 days. Cleveland Clinic notes you may not ovulate every month when cycles are unpredictable.

## Why estimates slip

Ovulation calculators assume a typical luteal phase. Early, late, or absent ovulation breaks that assumption.

## What still helps

- Log period starts for your clinician  
- Use [OPKs](/how-to-use-an-ovulation-test)  
- Track [cervical mucus](/cervical-mucus-and-fertility)  
- Chart [BBT](/basal-body-temperature-tracking) to confirm ovulation after the fact  
- Try the [calculator](/) as a rough map, then verify

## Possible contributors

Stress, weight changes, intense exercise, thyroid issues, PCOS, breastfeeding, and some medications can affect cycles. Only a clinician can diagnose causes.

## When to get care

Absent periods for months, extremely painful cycles, or unsuccessful trying on the timeline your clinician recommends for your age — seek evaluation. Do not use calendar estimates alone as birth control when cycles are unpredictable.
