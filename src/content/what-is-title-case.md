# What Is Title Case? Definition, Examples & Rules

**Title case** (also called **headline style**) is the capitalization style traditionally used for titles of books, movies, songs, plays, essays, and many headlines. In title case, major words are capitalized and minor words are lowercased.

![What is title case](/title-case-converter-search.webp)

## Simple definition

Capitalize every important word; leave small function words lowercase in the middle of the title.

Example: *Lord of the Flies* — *Lord* and *Flies* are major; *of* is a short preposition and stays lowercase.

## The everyday rules

1. Capitalize nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs  
2. Lowercase articles (*a*, *an*, *the*), many coordinating conjunctions, and short prepositions  
3. Always capitalize the first word — and in many styles, the last word  

That sounds easy until grammar interferes. The word *on* is a preposition in *Lay It All on Me* but an adjective in *It’s On Again*. Title case cares which job the word is doing.

## Title case vs capitalize each word

| Approach | Result for “the art of war” |
| --- | --- |
| Capitalize each word | *The Art Of War* (wrong — *Of* should be lowercase) |
| True title case | *The Art of War* |

Microsoft Word’s “Capitalize Each Word” and Excel’s `PROPER` are **not** true title case. Use a [title case converter](/) that understands style guides.

## Why style guides matter

There is no single “correct” title case. APA capitalizes longer prepositions; Chicago and MLA often leave more of them lowercase. Pick the guide your editor expects, then convert:

- AMA, AP, APA, Bluebook  
- Chicago, MLA, NY Times, Wikipedia
