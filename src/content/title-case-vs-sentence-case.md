# Title Case vs Sentence Case: What’s the Difference?

**Title case** and **sentence case** are the two capitalization styles people search for most when formatting headlines. Choosing the wrong one makes titles look inconsistent — or get rejected by an editor who expects a specific style guide.

## Quick definitions

**Title case** (headline style) capitalizes major words and leaves most minor words lowercase:

> The Mystery of the Missing Red Ruby

**Sentence case** capitalizes only the first word and proper nouns:

> The mystery of the missing red ruby

Title case is traditional for books, movies, songs, plays, and many essays or blog titles. Sentence case reads closer to ordinary prose and is popular for some news headlines, email subjects, and calm blog heading systems.

## When to use title case

Use title case when you need emphasis and formality:

- Book, movie, song, and album titles  
- Academic paper titles (often APA/Chicago/MLA title case)  
- Blog post titles when your brand prefers headline style  
- Product names and catalog headings that should “pop”  

Title case is also how many style guides tell you to refer to a work *inside* a sentence: *In Pride and Prejudice, Austen explores…*

## When to use sentence case

Use sentence case when you want a calmer, more conversational look:

- Some news headlines  
- Email subject lines  
- Blog H2/H3 headings on sites that prefer minimal capitals  
- UI labels that should read like normal sentences  

## Why sentence case is harder for software

Humans spot proper nouns easily. Computers do not. Names, places, brands, and event titles are endless — and many words are proper nouns only sometimes (*new* vs *New York*; *deal* vs *Green New Deal*). Title-case conversion, by contrast, follows more explicit style-guide lists and is usually more predictable.

## Style-guide shortcuts

| Goal | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| Academic / research titles | Title case (APA, Chicago, MLA, AMA, Bluebook…) |
| News composition titles | Title case with AP or NY Times rules |
| Everyday blog headings | Either — pick one house style |
| Reference-list entries (APA) | Sentence case for article/book titles in the list |
