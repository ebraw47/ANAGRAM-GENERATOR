# Best Wordle Starting Words (Ranked by Letter Coverage)

The best **Wordle starting word** is not a magic answer — it is a five-letter package of high-frequency letters that teaches you the most, on average, about the daily puzzle. This guide ranks practical openers the way ranking strategy pages do: unique letters, vowel/consonant balance, and coverage of common answer tiles.

## What makes a starting word “best”?

Strong openers usually:

- Use **five distinct letters** (no repeats on guess one)
- Mix **2–3 vowels** with high-value consonants
- Cover frequent tiles: **E A R O T L I S N C**
- Avoid ultra-rare letters early: **Q J X Z V**

Scientific and math-driven writeups often favor words like **SOARE**, **SLATE**, **CRANE**, and **TRACE** for information gain. Letter-coverage scoring on common answer lists often elevates **IRATE**, **LATER**, **ALTER**, and **ALERT**.

## Vowel-heavy vs. balanced openers

- **Vowel-heavy** (ADIEU, AUDIO, OUIJA): quickly reveal which vowels exist, but often waste a consonant slot and can leave awkward boards.
- **Balanced** (SLATE, CRANE, IRATE): usually better average information because consonants discriminate answers faster.

If your goal is the fewest average guesses, prefer balanced openers. If you personally hate missing vowels, a vowel-first word can still be valid — just follow it with a consonant-rich scout.

## Openers to avoid on guess one

- Words with **repeated letters** (EEEK vibes) — you test fewer unique tiles
- Obscure letter bombs (**XYLYL**, **QAJAQ**) — rare payoff
- Proper-feel quirks that are hard to build from later

## FAQ

**Is there one scientifically perfect Wordle starter?**  
No single word wins every metric. Entropy leaders and coverage leaders overlap but are not identical. Consistency beats perfection.

**Should I change starters for hard mode?**  
You can keep the same opener. Hard mode mainly changes guess two and later — see our [hard mode guide](/wordle-hard-mode-guide).

**Do starting words spoil today’s answer?**  
No. An opener is a probe, not a leak.
