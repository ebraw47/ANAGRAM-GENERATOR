# Sentence Case Converter: What It Is and When to Use It

A **sentence case converter** capitalizes text the way ordinary English sentences are capitalized: first word and proper nouns only. Everything else stays lowercase. It is the quieter twin of title case — and a frequent search for writers who need both styles.

![Sentence case converter](/title-case-converter-fast.webp)

## What sentence case looks like

| Input | Sentence case |
| --- | --- |
| THE RISE OF REMOTE WORK IN AUSTIN | *The rise of remote work in Austin* |
| how to build a content engine | *How to build a content engine* |
| bank of america reports earnings | *Bank of America reports earnings* |

Proper nouns (*Austin*, *Bank of America*) stay capitalized. Function words do not get title-case treatment.

## Why sentence case is hard for software

Humans spot proper nouns easily. Software must guess among endless names and context-dependent phrases (*new* vs *New York*, *green* vs *Green New Deal*). Our converter recognizes many common proper nouns, but expect occasional manual tweaks — more often than with title case.

## When sentence case wins

- Soft blog headings and documentation  
- Email subjects and notification copy  
- APA reference-list titles  
- News sites that prefer conversational headlines  

Use **title case** for book titles, composition titles, and punchy marketing H1s. Compare both in [title case vs sentence case](/title-case-vs-sentence-case).

## Sentence case vs title case at a glance

| | Title case | Sentence case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major words | Capitalized | Lowercase (except first / proper nouns) |
| Best for | Books, essays, many SEO titles | Emails, some news, APA references |
| Converter accuracy | Higher (rule-based) | Good, with occasional proper-noun fixes |
