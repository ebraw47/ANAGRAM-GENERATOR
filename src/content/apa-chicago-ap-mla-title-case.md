# APA vs Chicago vs AP vs MLA Title Case: Which Style Wins?

Writers search “APA title case,” “Chicago title case,” and “AP vs MLA capitalization” constantly because the guides disagree — especially on prepositions. This page is a practical side-by-side so you can pick a style and convert with confidence.

![APA Chicago AP MLA title case](/title-case-converter-accuracy.webp)

## One-line cheat sheet

| Guide | Prepositions (mid-title) | Last word rule | Typical audience |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **APA** | Lowercase ≤3 letters; capitalize longer (*With*, *From*) | Not forced | Psychology, education, social sciences |
| **AP** | Similar length cutoff for composition titles | Capitalize last word | Newsrooms, press releases |
| **Chicago** | Lowercase more short/medium prepositions | Capitalize last word | Books, many publishers |
| **MLA** | Lowercase **all** prepositions | Capitalize last word | Humanities, literature |

Also available on our tool: AMA, Bluebook, NY Times, and Wikipedia.

## Same title, four styles

Input: *a guide to writing with clarity from day one*

| Style | Typical result focus |
| --- | --- |
| APA / AP | *With* and *From* capitalized (4+ letters) |
| Chicago | More prepositions stay lowercase |
| MLA | *to*, *with*, *from* stay lowercase as prepositions |

## Which should you choose?

| Situation | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| Journal or university APA assignment | APA |
| Newspaper or PR headline | AP |
| Book manuscript / CMOS house style | Chicago |
| English / humanities paper | MLA |
| Medical / legal specialty | AMA or Bluebook |
| Wikipedia-style heading | Wikipedia |

If nobody specified a guide, pick **one** and stay consistent across the site. Mixed conventions look sloppier than either Chicago or APA alone.
